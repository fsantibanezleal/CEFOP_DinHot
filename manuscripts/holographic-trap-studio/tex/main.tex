\begin{abstract}
A phase-only spatial light modulator (SLM) in the pupil of a microscope objective can form hundreds to thousands of optical traps at once, but only if the phase pattern it displays is computed well: the pattern fixes how much light reaches the traps, how equal they are and how fast it can be recomputed. The largest published neutral-atom array holds \execAtoms{} atoms with a trap-depth spread of \execSpread{} percent. DinHot is an open studio that runs \nLadder{} methods (\nPublished{} published ones and \nProposed{} proposed here) on the same task, with the same seeds and metrics, on a scalar Fourier model with a device model (quantization, pixel crosstalk, aperture, flicker). The report gives the model, twelve data contracts, every method with equations, pseudocode, parameters and acceptance test, \nCases{} synthetic cases, a benchmark of \benchRows{} rows and two learned models. Over \hlN{} cases of the bake, among them a device-limited and an aberrated one, phase-fixed weighted Gerchberg--Saxton leaves a median relative standard deviation of \hlSigmaMseven{} percent (per-seed medians \hlSigmaMsevenLo{} to \hlSigmaMsevenHi{}) against \hlSigmaMfour{} percent (\hlSigmaMfourLo{} to \hlSigmaMfourHi{}) for plain Gerchberg--Saxton, means over \hlSeeds{} seeds, but its published freeze index stalls on a crowded array of this grid. Three of the proposed methods meet their criteria within a measured scope (a device-aware weighted update, a flicker-bounded phase-continuous sequence and an efficiency--uniformity front), one meets a narrowed criterion (an aberration-robust hologram) and one is refuted by a control (a learned warm start). A network trained on three-plane volumes beats its baseline at equal wall-clock on all \cghN{} held-out volumes (median accuracy ratio \cghMed{}), whereas its two-dimensional counterpart ties with Gerchberg--Saxton (ratio \cghTwoDMed{}). The model is a scalar Fourier model of a simulated device, not a calibrated instrument.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} holographic optical tweezers, spatial light modulator, weighted Gerchberg--Saxton, pixel crosstalk, neutral-atom arrays, hologram benchmarking, learned holography.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

The methods are described in the order of the design document's ladder: the published methods, from the superposition of prisms and lenses to the volumetric form of Gerchberg--Saxton, and then the methods proposed here, from the device-aware weighted GS to the efficiency--uniformity Pareto front; each subsection names its method. Every method is a module with a specification and a run function behind the interface of Section~\ref{sec:arch-engine}: it takes the optical setup, a target of traps or of a shaped field, an optional device, a budget, the portable random generator and an optional starting phase, and returns the phase, a history of the metrics, the trap fields and a metadata record. The forward model of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:delay} and~\eqref{eq:field} and the metrics of Eq.~\eqref{eq:metrics} are shared by all of them, and the scorer recomputes the scores from the returned phase, so that no method grades itself. Each subsection gives the role of the method and its source, the equations, the parameters with their values and how they were chosen, the computational complexity, the lane, the acceptance criterion with the gate that measures it and the measurement of this report, and the implementation module. Every method is a module of the engine of version \productVersion{}, the version of the product this report describes, and the module is named at the end of its subsection. The three closed-form methods (the superposition, the random superposition and the random mask) are given by their equations; every other method that runs as a procedure has its pseudocode, transcribed from the module (Algorithms~\ref{alg:gsfamily} to~\ref{alg:b5}), the plain, adaptive and weighted forms of Gerchberg--Saxton sharing one algorithm with the coefficient rule as a parameter. The measured text of each method is generated by the acceptance script and not typed here. The keyword defaults are read from the signature of each run function and are listed in Table~\ref{tab:defaults}; the acceptance criteria are in Table~\ref{tab:ladder}.
