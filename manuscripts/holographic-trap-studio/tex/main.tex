\begin{abstract}
A phase-only spatial light modulator (SLM) can form hundreds to thousands of optical traps at once. The largest published neutral-atom array holds \execAtoms{} atoms with a trap-depth spread of \execSpread{} percent. DinHot is an open studio that runs \nLadder{} methods (\nPublished{} published ones and \nProposed{} proposed here) on the same task, with the same seeds and metrics, on a scalar Fourier model with a device model. The report gives twelve data contracts, every method with its equations, \nCases{} cases and \benchRows{} benchmark rows. The benchmark gives every method a deadline of \dlReadS{} s; a row whose median wall clock exceeds \dlFactor{} times it (\dlOverRead{} of \dlRowsRead{} rows) is over budget and is never a best or a win. A cell is a win only with five seeds on each side, disjoint interquartile ranges and a gap of 0.01 percentage points: on the ideal device the published methods win \benchWinsIdealPublished{} and the proposed ones \benchWinsIdealProposed{} of \benchGeoIdeal{} geometries (those of the \dlWinIdealProposedMethods{}), and on a crosstalk device \benchWinsDevicePublished{} against \benchWinsDeviceProposed{} of \benchGeoDevice{} (those of the \dlWinDeviceProposedMethods{}, the device model with a weighted-GS warm start, which the warm-started loss of Schroff et al. matches), the other cells tying. Over \hlN{} baked cases, phase-fixed weighted Gerchberg--Saxton leaves a median spread of \hlSigmaMseven{} percent against \hlSigmaMfour{} percent for plain Gerchberg--Saxton. The aberration-robust hologram meets a narrowed criterion (a median ratio of \ext{ab}{ratioAllMed} over \ext{ab}{nSeedsAll} seeds), the continuation is not a Pareto front, the learned warm start is refuted by its control and is out of the benchmark, and the device-aware refinement is a negative result. A three-plane network beats its baseline at equal wall clock on all \cghN{} held-out volumes (median accuracy ratio \cghMed{}). The model is simulated, not a calibrated instrument.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} holographic optical tweezers, spatial light modulator, weighted Gerchberg--Saxton, pixel crosstalk, neutral-atom arrays, hologram benchmarking, learned holography.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

The methods are described in the order of the design document's ladder: the published methods, from the superposition of prisms and lenses to the volumetric form of Gerchberg--Saxton, and then the methods proposed here, from the device-aware conjugate-gradient refinement to the efficiency--uniformity continuation; each subsection names its method. Every method is a module with a specification and a run function behind the interface of Section~\ref{sec:arch-engine}: it takes the optical setup, a target of traps or of a shaped field, an optional device, a budget, the portable random generator and an optional starting phase, and returns the phase, a history of the metrics, the trap fields and a metadata record. The forward model of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:delay} and~\eqref{eq:field} and the metrics of Eq.~\eqref{eq:metrics} are shared by all of them, and the scorer recomputes the scores from the returned phase, so that no method grades itself. Each subsection gives the role of the method and its source, the equations, the parameters with their values and how they were chosen, the computational complexity, the lane, the acceptance criterion with the gate that measures it and the measurement of this report, and the implementation module. Every method is a module of the engine of version \productVersion{}, the version of the product this report describes, and the module is named at the end of its subsection. The three closed-form methods (the superposition, the random superposition and the random mask) are given by their equations; every other method that runs as a procedure has its pseudocode, transcribed from the module (Algorithms~\ref{alg:gsfamily} to~\ref{alg:b5}), the plain, adaptive and weighted forms of Gerchberg--Saxton sharing one algorithm with the coefficient rule as a parameter. The measured text of each method is generated by the acceptance script and not typed here. The keyword defaults are read from the signature of each run function and are listed in Table~\ref{tab:defaults}; the acceptance criteria are in Table~\ref{tab:ladder}.
