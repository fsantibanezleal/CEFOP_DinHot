\title{Device-Aware Conjugate-Gradient Refinement of Holographic Optical Trap Arrays: Neither the Efficiency-Level Loss, the Weight Update nor the Safeguard Improves on the Normalized-Error Loss of Schroff et al., and the Device Model in the Loop Carries the Gain, in Simulation}
\author{Felipe Santib\'a\~nez-Leal~\orcidlink{0000-0002-0150-3246}\\
\small CAOS open-research programme, Santiago, Chile\\
\small \texttt{fsantibanez@gmail.com}}
\date{}
\maketitle
\vspace{-1.2em}
\headerblock

\begin{abstract}
Arrays of optical traps are computed on an ideal model of the spatial light modulator (SLM) with phase-fixed weighted Gerchberg--Saxton (WGS), and the device then displays something else. Schroff et al.\ put a pixel-crosstalk model inside a conjugate-gradient minimization of a normalized intensity error. We transpose that approach to point-trap arrays as a device-aware conjugate-gradient refinement with an efficiency-level loss, an optional weight update and a best-iterate safeguard, and we test whether these elements add anything. Through the same warm start, device model and conjugate gradient, the normalized-error loss of Schroff et al.\ gives \schroffXSig{} percent against \ablBFullSig{} percent for the efficiency-level loss on a $10\times10$ lattice behind a crosstalk device, and a lower median on the full device (\schroffFSig{} against \ablBFFullSig{} percent), over \schroffXN{} seeds each: the efficiency-level loss adds nothing measurable, the safeguard did not act, and the weight update raises the spread \ablBWeightsRatio{} times. The gain belongs to the device model in the loop: the spread falls from a median of \sigmaMsevenThirty{} percent for phase-fixed WGS to \sigmaBOne{} percent, a factor of \ratioOneGm{} (95 percent interval \ratioOneCiLo{} to \ratioOneCiHi{}), and returns to \sigmaNoModel{} percent without the model. On the benchmark's device the gain is partial: at an equal wall-clock time of \benchOneTimeBudget{} s it reads as better on \benchOneDevTimeProposed{} of \benchOneDevCells{} geometries and ties on \benchOneDevTimeTie{}, and a hologram takes \benchTenSecRefMin{} to \benchTenSecRefMax{} s against \benchTenSecMsevenMin{} to \benchTenSecMsevenMax{} s. All results are simulated.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} holographic optical tweezers, weighted Gerchberg--Saxton, conjugate gradient, pixel crosstalk, phase quantization.
